Streetcars in Charlotte

Did you know? We had 35 miles of streetcar track in the early 1900s!

As Charlotte grew in the 1880s-1920s, a greater demand for transportation cryed out. Cars were not available or preferred by the public until the 1920s, so Streetcars began to be laid. The first streetcar company, Charlotte Street Railway company began service with horse/mule drawn trolleys in 1887 on Trade and Tryon streets outward to the railroad stations.
In 1890, Edward Dilworth Latta partnered with the Edison Electric Company to construct and operate a novel electric streetcar system. The first train left the square with Service to the new Latta Park in the newly developed neighborhood of Dilworth[1]. The Dilworth line was only the first of the streetcar Suburbs, and many other lines were constructed into other parts of of the growing new south city.

Operations reached a high point in the 1910s. The line mileage expanded from 4 miles in 1895 to 24.4 by 1913.That golden era seeing the Myers park neighborhood grow, with a line extending into it from the Elizabeth Lane line in 1912[2]. At its peak, there were ~50 miles of service under its operations by Duke Power Company(Now Duke Energy)[3].

The map below depicts the course of the lines as they existed at their peak extent in 1935, alongside the road network of the era.

Cracks in the system began to form as the streetcar workers clashed with the Southern Public Utilites company. These clashes resulted in higher operation costs and friction by the early 1920s. The personal automobile was also becoming more accessible, and while the public did enjoy the permance of rails, the convinence and flexibilty cars provided began to erode ridership first among the upper class, but later the middle and lower classes.
The streetcar retained it's appeal amongst commuting white collar workers even in the late 1920-30s as a way to "escape from traffic and the irritation of finding a parking space". By the 1930s, there was both corporate and political will to retire the fixed rail streetcar, with the sentiment being that the city had outgrown the "Rigid and noisy" mode. In 1937, an application by Duke Power Company and the City of charlotte was filed to the NC utilities Commision,which regulated such service at the time. This application requested permission to replace the streetcars with Motorbuses which were more flexible, safer, quieter and less capital intensive. This was granted, and in March 1938, Streetcar 85 made its last run to the S.Blvd barn[4].
