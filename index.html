The Crossroads of Charlotte

Welcome! This is strives to be a trove of Charlotte, and regional knowledge. It's primary focus is on history of the county of Mecklenburg and how it's present and future has been influenced by the past.

If you have wondered any of the following, this place is for you!

  • "Why are so few roads on a grid?"
  • "Why are the roads here so confusing?"
  • "How did I get from driving on one street, to another without making any turns?? I'm confused!"
  • "Why do so many roads names contain Ford, Ferry, Sharon or Queens?"
  • "Where is all the Charlotte history at? Did Charlotte just appear out of a vacuum last Thursday?"

The best place to start is The story of Trade and Tryon which gives useful backstory to Charlotte's founding story. It also helps you to begin to see why some roads are where they are. Much of our major roads throughout current Charlotte were defined in an era where Charlotte was a small village smaller than even the main uptown, without the design pressures of other east coast cities of the time, and hilly geography.

Alternately, if reading is more your thing, The story of Charlotte can give a abbreviated account of the city, which was founded by a man Named Thomas Polk, at the intersection of 2 Native American trading paths. Charlotte saw the first gold rush, ushered in a rail-powered cotton boom, all before becoming the second financial hub behind NYC, and growing rapidly over 120 years from 35k to 950k!

There is also a compendium of links that have been compiled, that were used in the making of the data for this site.

About the author


This project is led by Ben House, a full blooded Charlotte Native. He is a burgeoning historian, focusing on infrastructure's interplay with our living world, and how the quirks and oddities in our built landscape point to threads of our history. He has catalogued the age of nearly every road in Mecklenburg county: "The Map" He started Crossroads of Charlotte, orginially as the "Hornet's Nest Evolution Project" in 2024 by just wanting to understand why the roads of Charlotte were so strange. He is a lifelong resident of Charlotte, except for obtaining a Bachelor's in Atmospheric and Computer Science at UNC Asheville.
He hopes to shed light onto the never-ending why, of his often misunderstood hometown, that seems hell-bent to demolish it's past. Contact Ben by email at Ben@crossroadsofcharlotte.org. He is always looking to hear from from people about the subject, and where to expand this site towards!

The project is ongoing, with new data being pulled in from multiple sources for different time periods. I am starting as early back as I have good data for and working forward. Please keep checking back for new data to see how it evolves!

I am always looking for fellow appreciators of history interested in contributing to the project, whether that be via tracking down sources, pulling those into the map, or just suggestions. Get more info and reach out on the project's github by "opening an issue"!

Or if you want to just be notified when I publish a notable amount of new content, send me an email at info@crossroadsofcharlotte.org

The Crossroads of Charlotte is a citizen led, Geographic Information Systems (GIS) project dedicated to digitizing the historical evolution of Mecklenburg County, and surrounding areas, via physical infrastructure, primarily roads and railways, and other traces left in the present.

You can also support by buying me a coffee. As a token of gratitude I will prioritize researching a neighborhood of your choice, providing a quick report about it, and adding an info point crediting you as a patron

Thank you in advance for any and all support, even if it is just admiring the hidden history of Charlotte!
