Trade and Tyron: The Founding Myth

Why is the grid at an angle? What do we know?

Charlotte as a Euro American Settlement formally began in the 1760s as a small village in then the backcountry of a growing British Colony, when Thomas Polk who had been granted a large plot of land where Uptown Charlotte now sits. He had built a homestead at the crossroads of 2 intersecting trading paths orginally created by the Catawba and Cherokee who resided in the region before British colonization[1]. As the area became more populous, the need for a courthouse and government began to arise, and Thomas, along with Abraham Alexander and John Frolock took a leading role in that, and a courthouse for the county of Mecklenburg was built near his home, at that intersection.

As a part of the petition for chartering a town that they had high aspirations for, Polk commissioned a survey to establish a framework for future expansion. That survey was a square grid with the intersection of those 2 paths setting the orientation, at 47° East. While the exact orientations and paths of these are likely lost to history and development, the SW-NE path we call Tryon St took a route that followed an easy path along the tops of the hills leading towards a easier ford (a brigdeless crossing of water) of the Catawba River, it follows the ridge in a NE direction. The original route of the other is less clear today(more on that later.), but would have come SE to NW, crossing Little Sugar Creek, then up the rise, crossing Tyron, then crossing Irwin. The orginal area that charlotte annexed was 360 acres in size, which it remained until expansion during the gold Era of CLT in the 1830s [3].
